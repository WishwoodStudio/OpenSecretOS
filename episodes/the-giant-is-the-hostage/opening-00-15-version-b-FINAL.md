## Every wording change, one sentence each

1. **"of Microsoft's entire future cloud business" → "of Microsoft's entire future cloud revenue"** (VO only, on-screen card left as approved) — the actual claim is about contracted revenue, not the vaguer, broader notion of "business," so this is more factually precise.
2. **"The giant of the entire tech industry" → "A giant of the tech industry"** — drops the unverifiable implication that Microsoft is uniquely *the* giant of *the entire* industry, while keeping the load-bearing "giant" word the episode's own title depends on.
3. **"now hands its future to—" → "now hands almost half its cloud future to—"** — ties the claim back to the already-established 45% figure instead of vaguely implying the whole of Microsoft's corporate future is at stake, which the source material never claims.
4. **"The company it built." → "The company it helped fund."** (both VO and on-screen, since the original on-screen card repeated the same inaccuracy) — corrects a factual error: Microsoft invested in OpenAI, it did not found or build it, and the Editorial Board's own Concept A material independently establishes the investment framing this now matches.
5. **"But look at that number again." → "Now look at forty-five percent again."** — replaces a vague pronoun ("that number") with the concrete figure so the line is self-contained even without the visual, and replaces the argumentative connector "But" with the more neutral, factual pivot "Now."

## What did not change, and why

"—OpenAI." are untouched — each was already maximally clear, minimally
interpretive, and factually precise as written; editing them further
would have been change for its own sake, not improvement.

Both Board-specified on-screen hook cards (0:00–0:05.0: "45%" / "OF
MICROSOFT'S ENTIRE FUTURE CLOUD BUSINESS." / "ONE COMPANY.") are
unchanged, even though the VO now says "revenue" instead of "business" —
those cards are quoted directly from the Editorial Board's approved
concept, not this document's own wording, so they were treated as locked
rather than polished. Documentary Hybrid's own principle (typography
supports narration, it isn't required to mirror it word-for-word)
supports letting VO carry the more precise phrasing here.
