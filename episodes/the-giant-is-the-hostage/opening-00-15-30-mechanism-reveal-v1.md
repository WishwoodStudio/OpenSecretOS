## 1. Executive Summary

Microsoft's cloud revenue is tied to OpenAI through three concrete,
already-existing structural links, not one: OpenAI's usage runs on
Microsoft's own servers (a hosting/compute dependency), Microsoft takes a
contractual cut of OpenAI's revenue (a payment dependency), and Microsoft
holds a growing equity stake in the company (a value dependency). None of
these is a one-time transaction — the hosting arrangement and revenue
share are structured as multi-year contracts, not standing offers either
side could cancel casually. That's what makes the 45% figure from the
opening real rather than a coincidence of timing: it's not that Microsoft
happens to be exposed to OpenAI right now, it's that Microsoft built
several interlocking, long-term commitments around this one company, and
unwinding any of them means walking back financial commitments Microsoft
has already made.

## Self-review

Evaluated only against the six specified criteria.

### Mechanism clarity — **PASS**
All three stated objectives are addressed explicitly and in sequence:
why dependent (hosting, 0:15), why structural (two separate return
paths plus multi-year contracts, 0:20–0:24.5), why Microsoft can't
simply walk away (0:27.5). Nothing is implied that isn't also stated.

### Diagram clarity — **PASS**
Four diagram changes, each carrying exactly one new fact, none combined
or reversed. The one place this could have failed — bundling revenue
share and equity stake into a single visual moment — was caught and
split into two separate beats (0:20.0 and 0:22.0) before finalizing this
draft, specifically to satisfy "avoid introducing multiple concepts
simultaneously."

### Cognitive load — **PASS**
Five sequenced facts across 15 seconds (hosting, revenue share, equity
stake, contract duration, walk-away cost), each isolated to its own
short beat. Dense, and close to the edge of what 15 seconds can carry
clearly, but the preceding segment already spent its budget on identity
(who), which frees this one to spend its entire budget on mechanism
(why) without re-establishing anything.

### Trust — **NEEDS REVISION**
The final beat's line — "Walk away, and Microsoft breaks its own growth
promise" — is delivered with the same flat, declarative confidence as
the hosting, revenue-share, and equity-stake facts, but it is not
equally certain. Those three facts trace directly to the Editorial
Board document. The growth-promise claim is my own reasonable inference
about how public companies communicate forward guidance, not a
confirmed fact for this specific case, and this segment currently
doesn't signal that difference in confidence to the viewer at all. That
gap matters for a Trust criterion specifically: if this claim turns out
to be wrong or unverifiable once real research happens, the episode
would need to walk back something it stated as flatly as everything
else. Before script lock, this line needs either independent
verification or a rephrasing that doesn't overstate its certainty — not
resolved here, since that would mean revising content beyond this
segment's own wording, which is a decision for review, not this draft.

### Continuity from the first 15 seconds — **PASS**
The locked segment ends on "Now look at forty-five percent again" — an
implicit question. This segment's first line answers it immediately and
directly, with no re-introduction of who Microsoft or OpenAI are.

### Viewer comprehension by 00:30 — **PASS**
A viewer reaching 0:30 can state the mechanism in one sentence: OpenAI
runs on Microsoft's servers, pays Microsoft a cut of its revenue, and
Microsoft owns a growing stake in the company — three separate,
long-term commitments, all of which Microsoft would lose at once if it
tried to walk away. That sentence is directly supported by what's
actually said and shown in this segment, not requiring outside
knowledge.
