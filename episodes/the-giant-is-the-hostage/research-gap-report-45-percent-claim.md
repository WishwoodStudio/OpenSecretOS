# Research Gap Report — The 45% Claim

**Question investigated (one only, per instruction):** Why does the
Editorial Board conclude that approximately 45% of Microsoft's future
cloud revenue depends on OpenAI?

**Scope:** Searched only within the approved research package —
`episodes/OS_Research_MSFT_OpenAI_EditorialBoard_ConceptCompare_v1.md` —
and any primary sources it cites. No secondary explanations, no outside
knowledge, no invented reasoning. Confirmed no other MSFT/OpenAI research
document exists anywhere in this repository before concluding anything
(searched for the document by name, by topic, and by content pattern —
no matches beyond the one file).

---

## 1. Is the causal chain explicitly supported?

**No.** The document's own header states, verbatim: *"Exercise type:
Pure editorial comparison. No research performed. Both concepts assumed
fully factually defensible."*

The 45% figure appears three times in the document (the hook
specification, the personal-consequence line, and the head-to-head
comparison table), and in every instance it is used as a **given input**
to an editorial/creative decision — which hook pattern to use, which
personal-stake question to ask — never as a **conclusion** the document
itself derives from evidence. There is no sentence anywhere in the
document that explains why the figure is 45%, how it was calculated, or
what specifically is being measured (contracted backlog? analyst
projection? a specific disclosed segment?). It is asserted, not
established.

## 2. Which primary source supports it?

**None are cited in the available material.** A direct search of the
document for source markers (citations, footnotes, filing references,
URLs, "per [X]" attributions) returned nothing associated with the 45%
figure. No SEC filing, earnings call, analyst report, or company
statement is named anywhere in the document.

## 3. The exact missing link

The document itself names it, without providing it. Its closing footnote
reads: *"this is the third independent Board exercise — reveal-strength
scoring, primary-source-only fact audit, and now pure editorial
experience — to land on this same concept as the stronger candidate,
each time by a different method."*

This is a direct reference to a **separate, prior Editorial Board
exercise specifically described as "primary-source-only"** — meaning an
exercise that, unlike this one, was source-grounded. That document is
where the actual sourcing for the 45% figure (or the reveal concept more
broadly) would be expected to live. It does not exist anywhere in this
repository — confirmed by searching for it directly (by name pattern,
by topic, and by content) with no results. The missing link is not a
vague "more research needed" — it is this specific, named, referenced
document that the approved research package itself points to and that
was not included in what's available here.

## 4. Recommendation: **Research incomplete**

Not "research sufficient" — there is no cited evidence at all for the
central figure the entire episode is built around. Not "editorial claim
should be weakened," either — weakening presumes there is some real, if
thin, evidence to soften a claim relative to. That's not this situation:
there is zero evidence in hand, not weak evidence. It's unknown from the
available material whether 45% is accurate, too high, too low, or
measuring something other than what the hook implies. Weakening the
language now would be guessing at a hedge without knowing what needs
hedging.

**What would resolve this:** locating and reviewing the "primary-source-
only fact audit" the current document references, or an equivalent
sourced document for this specific figure. Until one of those exists in
this repository, the causal chain behind the episode's central statistic
is unverified, not just unstated.
