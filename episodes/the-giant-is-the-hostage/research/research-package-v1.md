### RP-GIANT-001 (= RFA-GIANT-001)
**Claim text:** "Approximately 45% of Microsoft's future cloud revenue depends on OpenAI."
**Primary source:** Microsoft FY26 Q2 earnings call (January 28, 2026).
CFO Amy Hood, quoted directly: *"Approximately 45% of our commercial RPO
balance is from OpenAI."* ([transcript via The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/01/28/microsoft-msft-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/);
corroborated independently by [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/nearly-half-of-microsofts-commercial-contract-backlog-is-tied-to-openai/)
and [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/microsoft-says-openai-driving-45-014014164.html)).
**Note:** the sourced figure is 45% of Microsoft's **commercial Remaining
Performance Obligations (RPO)** — total contracted-but-unrecognized
revenue across Microsoft's whole commercial business — not "cloud
revenue" specifically. RPO is heavily cloud/Azure-driven in this case
(OpenAI's contribution stems substantially from its $250B Azure
commitment, see RP-GIANT-005), but RPO and "cloud revenue" are distinct
accounting terms. The claim as worded is more specific than what the
primary source actually states.

### RP-GIANT-002 (= RFA-GIANT-002)
**Claim text:** "Microsoft's cloud revenue from this relationship is contracted to be earned for years into the future."
**Primary source:** Same earnings call — Microsoft's commercial RPO
carries a *"weighted average duration of approximately 2.5 years"* ([The
Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/01/28/microsoft-msft-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).
Separately, OpenAI's revenue-share obligation to Microsoft is reported to
extend *"through 2030"* and its IP license terms run to 2032 ([om.co](https://om.co/2026/05/01/what-microsofts-10-q-says-about-openai/);
[Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/28/openai-for-profit-restructuring-microsoft-stake/)).
**Note:** the 2.5-year figure is a blended average across Microsoft's
entire commercial RPO, not an OpenAI-specific duration figure. The
2030/2032 dates are OpenAI-specific but come from secondary reporting on
contract terms, not a primary filing I was able to access directly (see
Research Notes).

### RP-GIANT-003 (= RFA-GIANT-003)
**Claim text:** "Microsoft's own quarterly profit swings by billions of dollars purely on OpenAI's fortunes."
**Primary source:** Microsoft's own FY26 Q1 earnings disclosure: net
income and diluted EPS were reduced by **$3.1 billion and $0.41
respectively** due to equity-method losses on the OpenAI investment, up
from **$523 million** the prior year ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/29/microsoft_earnings_q1_26_openai_loss/);
[Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/artificial-intelligence/openai-chatgpt/microsoft-obscures-openais-usd11-5-billion-loss)).
**Note:** directly supported, with an actual dollar figure and
year-over-year comparison, both attributed to Microsoft's own disclosed
numbers.

### RP-GIANT-004 (= RFA-GIANT-004)
**Claim text:** "ChatGPT has to keep paying [Microsoft] to keep running" (ongoing, usage-based compute cost).
**Primary source:** Microsoft's official blog, joint statement with
OpenAI (February 27, 2026): *"Azure remains the exclusive cloud provider
of stateless OpenAI APIs."* ([blogs.microsoft.com](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/02/27/microsoft-and-openai-joint-statement-on-continuing-partnership/))
**Note:** confirms OpenAI's API-serving infrastructure runs on Azure,
which supports the underlying relationship this claim describes. The
specific phrasing "has to keep paying to keep running" is interpretive,
not quoted language from either company.

### RP-GIANT-005 (= RFA-GIANT-005)
**Claim text:** "Microsoft owns the machines/infrastructure that OpenAI's services run on."
**Primary source:** Same Microsoft blog post: *"Azure remains the
exclusive cloud provider of stateless OpenAI APIs."* Also: OpenAI
"contracted to purchase an incremental $250 billion of Azure services"
as part of the October 2025 recapitalization ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/28/openai-for-profit-restructuring-microsoft-stake/)).
**Note:** well-supported for OpenAI's stateless API workloads
specifically. **Important qualifier the same source discloses:** the
October 2025 restructuring explicitly removed *"Microsoft's right of
first refusal to be OpenAI's compute provider"* — meaning exclusivity
has narrowed, not strengthened, over time, and the claim as broadly
worded ("the machines... run on") overstates a relationship that
Microsoft's own more recent statements describe as narrower and
non-exclusive outside of stateless API calls.

### RP-GIANT-006 (= RFA-GIANT-006)
**Claim text:** "Microsoft takes a cut of OpenAI's revenue."
**Primary source:** Microsoft's official February 27, 2026 blog post:
*"The ongoing revenue share arrangement remains unchanged and has always
included sharing revenue from partnerships between OpenAI and other
cloud providers."* Secondary reporting citing Microsoft's 10-Q adds a
specific figure: *"OpenAI will continue to pay Microsoft a 20 percent
revenue share through 2030, but only up to a fixed cap"* ([om.co](https://om.co/2026/05/01/what-microsofts-10-q-says-about-openai/)).
**Note:** the underlying fact (a revenue share exists) is directly
confirmed by Microsoft's own statement. The specific 20%/2030/cap figures
come from a secondary source's reporting on the 10-Q, not verified by me
against the primary filing directly — see Research Notes.

### RP-GIANT-007 (= RFA-GIANT-007)
**Claim text:** "Microsoft owns a stake in OpenAI now worth many times its original investment."
**Primary source:** Microsoft's 10-Q (fiscal quarter ended March 31,
2026), as reported: ~27% ownership stake, equity-method accounted; per
Fortune's reporting on the October 2025 recapitalization terms, that
stake was valued at approximately **$135 billion** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/28/openai-for-profit-restructuring-microsoft-stake/)).
**Note:** $135B against a $13B total investment is roughly 10.4x —
directly supports "many times." The $135B figure is secondary reporting
on the deal terms, not independently confirmed by me against a primary
filing.

### RP-GIANT-008 (= RFA-GIANT-008)
**Claim text:** "Microsoft's original investment in OpenAI was $13,000,000,000."
**Primary source:** Microsoft's own 10-Q, as reported consistently across
multiple independent secondary sources citing it: *"Total funding
commitment is $13 billion, of which $11.8 billion has been funded as of
March 31, 2026,"* composed of $1B (2019) + $10B (January 2023) + $2B

[om.co](https://om.co/2026/05/01/what-microsofts-10-q-says-about-openai/)).
**Note:** well-corroborated across multiple independent sources citing
the same filing. I was not able to load the SEC EDGAR filing directly
(blocked, HTTP 403) or Microsoft's investor-relations document mirror
(request timed out) to verify this figure against the primary document
myself — see Research Notes.

**Primary source:** Not independently sourced. This claim is a

### RP-GIANT-010 (= RFA-GIANT-010)
**Claim text:** "Walk away, and Microsoft breaks its own growth promise [to investors]."
**Primary source:** None found. No search returned any statement — from
Microsoft, from an analyst, or from any earnings disclosure — describing
a specific growth commitment Microsoft has made to investors that is
contingent on the OpenAI relationship.
**Note, stated plainly rather than left neutral:** what I found points
the other way. On the same FY26 Q2 earnings call where the 45% figure
was disclosed, CFO Amy Hood's own framing emphasized diversification and
resilience — *"55%... is related to the breadth of our portfolio... That
is a significant RPO balance, larger than most peers, more diversified
than most peers"* — when directly asked about concentration risk. That is
Microsoft's own public position actively pushing back against the
"can't afford to let go" framing, not evidence supporting it. This claim
is not weakened here to a softer version — it is reported as unsourced,
with the one relevant primary statement I found leaning against it.
