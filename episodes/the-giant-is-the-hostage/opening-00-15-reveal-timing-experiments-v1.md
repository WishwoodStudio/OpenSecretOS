# Reveal-Timing Experiments — Opening 00:00–00:15

**Episode:** The Giant Is The Hostage | **Concept:** Concept B (unchanged
across all three). **Single variable:** when OpenAI is named. Everything
else — the hook, the 45% figure, the "giant" framing, the visual
language — is held constant so the comparison is actually testing one
thing. Each version below is a complete, independent 15-second structure
in its own right, not a diff against the others.

---

**Editorial character:** reveal-then-recontextualize. All facts on the
table by 0:06.5; the remaining ~8.5 seconds are spent building the irony
of the relationship rather than sustaining mystery about identity.

### Evaluation

- **Scroll stopping:** identical to B and C — the 0:00–0:05 hook is
  unchanged across all three versions, and scroll-stopping is decided
  inside that window. This category is a wash between all three; it does
  not distinguish them.
- **Prior belief destruction:** **strongest of the three.** By spending
  8.5 of the 15 seconds on irony rather than suspense, this version gets
  closest to actually landing "it's backwards" within the segment,
  though still short of the full mechanism explanation.
- **Curiosity:** **weakest of the three.** The open loop closes at 0:05
  and never reopens — nothing carries the viewer past 0:15 except the
  irony itself, not an unresolved question.
- **Cognitive load:** moderate-high — two named entities plus an irony
  frame inside 15 seconds — but delivered as one linear escalation rather
  than layered mystery, which keeps it organized despite the density.
- **Retellability:** strong, and *complete* — "Microsoft built OpenAI and
  now depends on it for half its cloud future" is a full, satisfying
  retelling on its own, with nothing missing.

### Evaluation

- **Scroll stopping:** wash, same reasoning as Version A.
- **Prior belief destruction:** moderate-strong. Roughly 7 seconds of
  irony-building after the reveal (0:08–0:15) — slightly less runway than
  Version A's 8.5, but the belief is still visibly cracked, not just
  installed, by 0:15.
- **Curiosity:** **strongest of the three that still resolves within the
  segment.** The mid-sentence hold ("now hands its future to—") is a
  genuine, if brief, suspense beat — the viewer is held in an unresolved
  clause for about a second and a half before the payoff. That's real
  tension, deliberately short.
- **Cognitive load:** similar total information to Version A, but chunked
  more clearly (setup, suspense, payoff, irony as four distinct beats
  rather than two dense ones) — the structure itself may reduce felt load
  even though the raw information is comparable.
- **Retellability:** strong, and additionally *quotable* — "the giant
  hands its future to—" as a held clause is the kind of specific beat a
  viewer might repeat verbatim, not just paraphrase.

### Evaluation

- **Scroll stopping:** wash, same reasoning as A and B.
- **Prior belief destruction:** **weakest of the three.** No irony beat

- **Curiosity:** **strongest of the three, by construction.** The open

  carried into whatever comes next.
- **Cognitive load:** **lowest of the three** — one named entity, one

- **Retellability:** strong, but of a different *kind* than A or B — this
  is a teaser-retelling ("you won't believe what Microsoft depends on for
  half its business") rather than an insight-retelling. Effective for
  driving someone else to watch; less effective as a complete idea
  someone could repeat and have it land on its own.

## Recommendation: **Version B**

Not the safest middle ground chosen by default — three reasons, in order
of weight:

1. **It directly addresses the specific weakness the prior gate flagged.**
   The earlier self-review marked "Prior belief destruction" as NEEDS
   REVISION for the withhold-to-the-end structure (Version C), precisely
   because no irony lands within 15 seconds. Version B lands real irony
   by 0:15 while Version A does too — but B does it without paying
   Version A's full cost to curiosity.
2. **It's the best fit for the canonical retention principle already in
   force.** `Visual Identity System v2` §5: *"The first 3 seconds do not
   explain. They stop the scroll. The explanation begins only after
   attention is secured."* Version A compresses hook and explanation
   almost into the same breath (mystery closes at 0:05, irony fully
   stated by 0:09); Version B honors a real gap between "attention
   secured" and "explanation begins," then still delivers the explanation
   inside the segment.
3. **The suspense beat is the strongest single moment across all three
   versions.** "Now hands its future to—" is a specific, quotable,
   repeatable line in a way neither A's immediate reveal nor C's fully
   open loop produces.

**What Version B gives up, stated plainly:** it does not maximize any
single category — Version A destroys the prior belief slightly more
completely, Version C preserves more raw curiosity. B is the strongest
version by design, not the strongest on every individual axis, and that
trade-off is the actual recommendation, not a hedge.
