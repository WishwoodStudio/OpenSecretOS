## 1. Executive Summary

- **Working title:** Why Burberry Burned $38,000,000 Of Its Own Clothes
- **Final hook:** "Burberry burned £28.6 million of its own clothes." (exact, no credibility qualifier attached — the annual report appears later, as evidence, at the moment the viewer would reasonably start questioning the claim, not as a hedge on the opening line)
- **Hidden mechanism:** A discounted unit doesn't just lose money on that unit — it's a public price signal to every full-price customer that the whole line is worth less. Above a certain volume, that signal costs more than the unsold item. Destroying it is the cheaper option.
- **Reveal type:** Inversion — the apparently irrational act (destroying valuable inventory) is the financially rational one once the full signal cost is counted.
- **Mechanism name (≤3 words, spoken + on-screen at the reveal):** **The Markdown Tax**
- **Transfer lens:** Any brand selling status is running this same math. A public discount isn't a local price cut — it's a statement about what the rest of the line is worth. When that statement costs more than the item, brands find other ways to avoid saying it out loud: buybacks, gated resale, deliberately small restocks.
- **Personal stake (concrete, required by Constitution v4 §10 T5):** The viewer gains a reusable interpretive lens, not a warning — the next time a brand protects scarcity (a members-only resale channel, a deliberately tiny restock, a waitlist), they can ask whether they're looking at exclusivity or the same markdown-tax logic, just paid in a different currency (attention, patience, access). A changed default for reading brand-scarcity marketing, not an accusation aimed at the viewer.
- **Estimated runtime:** ~75 seconds (Duration Philosophy — no fixed target; length driven by payoff density and the personal-consequence beat, not a timestamp; well under the ≤3 min Shorts ceiling)

## 2. Final Script

> Burberry burned £28.6 million of its own clothes.
>
> Not a one-off write-down. Every year, same line: "finished goods physically destroyed." Ninety million pounds, over five years.
>
> And Burberry wasn't the only one. Cartier's parent company, Richemont, spent 481 million euros buying back its own unsold watches — then took them apart, piece by piece.
>
> Two different companies. Same decision: zero dollars, on purpose, instead of a discount. Why?
>
> Because a marked-down coat in an outlet bin isn't one lost sale. It's a public price tag — visible to every full-price customer — for the entire collection.
>
> So it's never "destroy it" versus "sell it for something." It's "destroy it" versus "quietly discount everything else, too." Count it that way, and zero is the cheaper number. This is the markdown tax.
>
> Burberry got caught and stopped, in 2018. France banned the practice outright, in 2022. You can regulate the method. You can't regulate the math.
>
> So next time a luxury brand offers you a "members-only" resale channel, or keeps a restock deliberately tiny — there's a different way to read it.
>
> It's not exclusivity built for you. It's the markdown tax, paid in a different currency — your attention, your patience, the appeal of being let in.
>
> So the next time you see a brand protecting its scarcity — a waitlist, a limited drop, an "invite-only" anything — ask yourself: is that exclusivity? Or is it the markdown tax?

**Word count:** ~215 words (shorter hook, reworked closing). **Fast, conversational pacing** — timing below is a planning estimate; lock final timecodes against ElevenLabs word-level timestamps once VO is generated (per canonical sequence: shot list first, voice second).

## 15. Editorial Improvements

**What changed compared to the previous package:**
- Personal Stake is now an explicit, standalone two-part beat (8a/8b) rather than absent. v1 ended on a generalized transfer lens with no concrete consequence for the viewer.
- Rolling Payoffs restructured: v1's single 12-second explain-then-reveal block (0:33–0:45) is now split into distinct payoff beats (question → signal reframe → named reveal), each under 10 seconds, per the Rolling Payoff Rule.
- The mechanism now has a name — "the markdown tax" — spoken at the reveal, shown on screen in the purple moment, echoed in the diagram title, the closing line, and the metadata/hashtags.
- A redundant beat (v1's standalone "scale" card) was cut and folded into the evidence beat it was repeating, per the instruction to remove beats that only restate information.
- The old fixed 58–59s runtime target is dropped in favor of Duration Philosophy; runtime grew to ~75s specifically to make room for the above, not through padding.

**Why these changes should improve retention:** the Rolling Payoff restructuring means no 10+ second span passes without a fresh hook for attention — the single long explanation block in v1 was the one place in that script most likely to lose a viewer mid-thought.

**Why these changes should improve memorability:** a named, repeatable mechanism ("the markdown tax") gives the audience a portable phrase to carry into comments and future episodes, in place of v1's un-named "math flips" description, which had nothing to hold onto after the video ended.

**Why these changes should improve audience attachment:** the personal-stake beat converts the episode from "here's an interesting fact about two companies" into "here's a lens you now have," which is the difference between passive information and something the viewer can use the next time they encounter a controlled-resale or restock offer.

**Trade-offs introduced:**
- Runtime is ~30% longer than v1 (75s vs. 58s) — deliberate, and still far under the 3-minute ceiling, but it does mean a slightly higher production and editing load (one additional AI block).
- The personal-stake beat carries real tone risk (see Production Risks) — it is the first beat in either version of this episode that speaks directly to the viewer's own behavior, which is exactly where the Constitution's "engineered, not victim" line is easiest to cross by accident.
- Naming the mechanism mid-script is a small bet: it works if it lands as insight, and reads as a slogan if the pacing is off — this is why it has its own line in the Voice Package and its own QA check.
