# Shot List — Luxury Destruction

**Director Package artifact.** Combines the source Production Package's
Beat Sheet (§3), Shot Package (§5), and AI Generation Package block
references (§6) into one shot-by-shot plan. Camera language and
continuity below are extracted from the source's own already-authored
creative decisions — not independently generated against
`canonical/visual-identity.md`, which does not exist in this repository.

Rolling Payoff Rule (as stated by the source): no gap exceeds ~10 seconds
without a contradiction, quantified insight, mini-reveal, reversal,
connection, or practical implication.

---

## Beat 1 — Hook

- **VO:** "Burberry burned £28.6 million of its own clothes."
- **Duration:** 0:00–0:05
- **Shot objective:** stop the scroll with an impossible number.
- **Camera language:** static frame, number centered; 2–3% push-in on
  background.
- **Visual block:** Block A (boutique interior).
- **Transition in:** cold open.
- **Continuity:** establishes Block A's color temperature — must be
  visually rhymed by Block E at the close (see Beat 9).
- **Payoff type:** quantified insight. No credibility qualifier attached
  to the number; the evidence for it arrives in Beat 2, at the point a
  viewer would reasonably start to doubt it.

## Beat 2 — Establish (scale)

- **VO:** "Not a one-off write-down. Every year, same line: 'finished
  goods physically destroyed.' Ninety million pounds, over five years."
- **Duration:** 0:05–0:11
- **Shot objective:** prove the claim is the company's own words.
- **Camera language:** static, zoom into report line; 3–5% push-in over
  5s.
- **Visual:** Real artifact 1 — Burberry Annual Report, cropped to the
  disclosure line.
- **Transition in:** hard cut.
- **Payoff type:** contradiction — this is a policy, not an accident.

## Beat 3 — Evidence (pattern)

- **VO:** "And Burberry wasn't the only one. Cartier's parent company,
  Richemont, spent 481 million euros buying back its own unsold watches —
  then took them apart, piece by piece."
- **Duration:** 0:11–0:20
- **Shot objective:** prove it's a pattern, not an anomaly.
- **Camera language:** static, zoom into report figure; mirrors Beat 2's
  push-in grammar (deliberate visual rhyme).
- **Visual:** Real artifact 2 — Richemont 2018 earnings report, p.3.
- **Transition in:** hard cut.
- **Continuity:** must use a distinct verb/visual metaphor from Beat 2 —
  "took apart," not "burned" — Richemont's mechanism (buyback +
  disassembly) must not be conflated with Burberry's (incineration).
- **Payoff type:** connection.

## Beat 4 — Explain (the question)

- **VO:** "Two different companies. Same decision: zero dollars, on
  purpose, instead of a discount. Why?"
- **Duration:** 0:20–0:25
- **Shot objective:** hold tension on the unanswered question.
- **Camera language:** static AI background, static hold — stillness as
  weight.
- **Visual block:** Block B (stockroom).
- **Transition in:** hard cut.
- **Payoff type:** reversal — sets up the unanswered tension.

## Beat 5 — Explain (the signal)

- **VO:** "Because a marked-down coat in an outlet bin isn't one lost
  sale. It's a public price tag — visible to every full-price customer —
  for the entire collection."
- **Duration:** 0:25–0:33
- **Shot objective:** land the reframe (discount = signal).
- **Camera language:** static hold, minimal drift, continuing Block B.
- **Visual block:** Block B (stockroom), continued — internal hard cut,
  no new import.
- **Payoff type:** mini-reveal — reframes "discount" as "public signal."

## Beat 6 — Reveal (named mechanism)

- **VO:** "So it's never 'destroy it' versus 'sell it for something.'
  It's 'destroy it' versus 'quietly discount everything else, too.'
  Count it that way, and zero is the cheaper number. This is the
  markdown tax."
- **Duration:** 0:33–0:44
- **Shot objective:** land the named mechanism.
- **Camera language:** diagram build, no camera movement (2D asset);
  build-in per element, 200–300ms each.
- **Visual block:** Block C (diagram stage) + Diagram Package ("The
  Markdown Tax" comparison diagram — see `asset-manifest.md`).
- **Transition in:** hard cut.
- **Continuity:** **purple moment**, ~0:41–0:44 — the single use of
  purple (`#A855F7`) in the entire episode, reserved exclusively for this
  on-screen mechanism name.
- **Payoff type:** main reveal — mechanism named on screen and spoken
  simultaneously.

## Beat 7 — Evidence (consequence)

- **VO:** "Burberry got caught and stopped, in 2018. France banned the
  practice outright, in 2022. You can regulate the method. You can't
  regulate the math."
- **Duration:** 0:44–0:52
- **Shot objective:** show consequence without adding new asset weight.
- **Camera language:** static AI background, typography only, no
  push-in.
- **Visual block:** Block D (storefront exterior, dusk).
- **Transition in:** hard cut.
- **Payoff type:** practical implication — regulation caught the method,
  not the underlying incentive.

## Beat 8a — Anchor (personal stake setup)

- **VO:** "So next time a luxury brand offers you a 'members-only' resale
  channel, or keeps a restock deliberately tiny — there's a different way
  to read it."
- **Duration:** 0:52–0:59
- **Shot objective:** deliver the personal-stake reversal (setup half).
- **Camera language:** static AI background, typography only, static
  hold.
- **Visual block:** Block E (closing interior).
- **Transition in:** hard cut.
- **Continuity:** this beat and 8b are the highest tone-risk point in the
  episode — the VO must read as a reinterpretation ("you're not looking
  at X, you're looking at Y"), not an accusation aimed at the viewer.
- **Payoff type:** mental-model setup, not a warning.

## Beat 8b — Anchor (personal stake payoff)

- **VO:** "It's not exclusivity built for you. It's the markdown tax,
  paid in a different currency — your attention, your patience, the
  appeal of being let in."
- **Duration:** 0:59–1:05
- **Shot objective:** deliver the personal-stake reversal (payoff half).
- **Camera language:** static hold, slight slow push-in (2%) beginning
  at 8b — marks the payoff.
- **Visual block:** Block E, continued.
- **Payoff type:** personal stake lands — a concrete changed default,
  framed as reinterpretation, not accusation.

## Beat 9 — Anchor (reusable mental model / close)

- **VO:** "So the next time you see a brand protecting its scarcity — a
  waitlist, a limited drop, an 'invite-only' anything — ask yourself: is
  that exclusivity? Or is it the markdown tax?"
- **Duration:** 1:05–1:16
- **Shot objective:** close on stillness, reinforce the mechanism name.
- **Camera language:** static wide hold, continuing into the logo card;
  one 200ms cross-dissolve into the logo.
- **Visual block:** Block E, continued, closing hold.
- **Continuity:** Block E's color temperature must match Block A's
  (bookend rhyme, opened in Beat 1).
- **Payoff type:** generalization as an open question — left for the
  viewer, not stated as a conclusion.
