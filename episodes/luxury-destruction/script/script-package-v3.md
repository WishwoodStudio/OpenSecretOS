## Final Script (v3)

> Burberry burned £28.6 million of its own clothes.
>
> Every year: "finished goods physically destroyed." Ninety million
> pounds, over five years.
>
> Burberry wasn't alone. Cartier's parent company, Richemont, spent 481
> million euros buying back its own unsold watches — then took them
> apart.
>
> Two different companies. Same decision: zero dollars, on purpose,
> instead of a discount. Why?
>
> Because a marked-down coat isn't one lost sale. It's a public price tag
> — visible to every full-price customer — for the entire collection.
>
> So it's never "destroy it" versus "sell it for something." It's
> "destroy it" versus "quietly discount everything else, too." Count it
> that way, and zero is the cheaper number. This is the markdown tax.
>
> Burberry stopped in 2018; France banned it in 2022.
>
> So the next time a brand protects its scarcity — a waitlist, a restock
> kept deliberately tiny — there's a different way to read it. It's not
> exclusivity built for you. It's the markdown tax, paid in a different
> currency: your attention, your patience, the appeal of being let in.
>
> Is that exclusivity? Or is it the markdown tax?

## Removal / compression log

Every change from v2, in order, with the reason it was judged non-essential
to the mechanism, the fact record, or the approved editorial direction.

1. **Beat 2 — cut "Not a one-off write-down."** (4 words)
   Redundant with the next clause, "Every year, same line" — both exist
   only to establish recurrence, and the second one already does it while
   also carrying the sourced figure. Not a fact itself, not mechanism
   content.

2. **Beat 2 — cut "same line"** (2 words)
   Same reasoning as #1: "Every year" already implies recurrence; "same
   line" was a second, unnecessary restatement of it. The quoted
   disclosure text itself ("finished goods physically destroyed") and the
   £90M/5-year figure are both preserved verbatim — the sourced content
   is untouched.

3. **Beat 3 — cut "And ... wasn't the only one" → "wasn't alone"** (3
   words)
   Pure phrasing compression. The pattern-not-anomaly point this sentence
   exists to make is fully preserved; only the wording is tightened.

4. **Beat 3 — "Cartier's parent company," — cut, then restored.**
   Originally cut for 3 words. Final editorial review (2026-07-12) flagged
   this as the one cut in the pass that risked harming understanding more
   than it saved runtime: beat 3's function is to prove the mechanism is
   a pattern, not a one-off, and that proof depends on the audience
   recognizing Richemont as a comparable luxury player — without "Cartier's
   parent company," an unrecognized holding-company name doesn't register
   as pattern evidence. Restored in full. Offset by item 10 below.

5. **Beat 3 — cut "piece by piece"** (3 words)
   Descriptive flourish on top of the required verb. The QA-mandated
   distinction from Burberry's method (§ Production Risks: "took them
   apart," not "burned") is preserved exactly — only the trailing
   embellishment is cut.

6. **Beats 8a/8b/9 — merged into one beat, cut 23 of 80 words**
   The largest single change, and the most load-bearing to document
   carefully. v2's own "Editorial Improvements" section (§15) states the
   Personal Stake beat was added *new* in v2 to satisfy the Personal
   Consequence gate — it did not exist in v1 as three separate beats. As
   written in v2, beats 8a, 8b, and 9 make the same point three times
   with two different, non-overlapping example lists (8a: "members-only
   resale channel," "restock deliberately tiny"; 9: "waitlist," "limited
   drop," "invite-only"). None of these examples are sourced facts —
   they're illustrative categories. This pass keeps exactly one example
   set (folding "waitlist" in, since it reads as the most concrete),
   keeps the personal-stake payoff line that calls back to the named
   mechanism ("paid in a different currency"), and keeps — verbatim, with
   only "So the next time you see" trimmed to fit — the closing question
   that v2's own Voice Package pacing notes single out as *"the line most
   likely to be quoted in comments."* The personal stake beat itself
   (the approved editorial addition from v2) is preserved; only its
   triple restatement is compressed to one.

7. **Beat 5 — cut "in an outlet bin"** (4 words)
   The mechanism claim ("a marked-down coat isn't one lost sale, it's a
   public price tag ... for the entire collection") is preserved in full,
   word for word except this one descriptive location detail. The
   mechanism does not depend on where the markdown happens — only that it
   is discounted and visible — so this is scene-setting, not mechanism
   logic.

8. **Beat 7 — cut "You can regulate the method. You can't regulate the
   math."** (10 words)
   The two verified facts in this beat — Burberry stopped in 2018, France
   banned the practice in 2022 — are preserved exactly. This sentence is
   the one clearly identifiable line in the whole script that is neither
   a sourced fact nor part of the mechanism explanation (the mechanism
   was already fully delivered in beat 6): it's a standalone editorial
   thesis-restatement layered on top of the two facts. It is the single
   largest word-count cut relative to how little mechanism or fact
   content it carried.

9. **Beat 7 — cut "outright"** (1 word)
   Emphasis-only modifier on "banned"; the fact (France banned the
   practice, 2022) is unchanged.

10. **Beat 7 — cut "got caught and"** (3 words), added 2026-07-12 to
    offset restoring item 4.
    "Burberry got caught and stopped in 2018" → "Burberry stopped in
    2018." The two verified facts (Burberry stopped in 2018, France
    banned it in 2022) are both fully intact — only "got caught," a
    narrative implication about *why* Burberry stopped (external
    enforcement) rather than a sourced claim itself, is removed. This
    beat is post-reveal consequence, not mechanism, reveal, or personal
    stake — chosen specifically because it was the lowest-information
    remaining segment outside those three protected areas: beat 2
    (scale) and beat 4 (the question) were already reviewed and judged
    load-bearing enough to leave alone.

**Not touched, at all:**
