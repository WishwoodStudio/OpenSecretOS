## SECTION 1 — Visual Philosophy

**Analytical.** Every visual decision communicates that someone actually thought about what this information means — not just illustrated it.

**Authoritative without being institutional.** Reference: smart financial-press publication (The Economist data graphics, FT visual journalism). Not a textbook, not a corporate deck.

**Quiet confidence.** No hype energy. No urgency animations. The mechanisms are compelling on their own terms.

**Earned revelation.** The viewer is seeing something previously hidden — not secret in a conspiratorial sense, but obscured by surface appearances.

### What Open Secret should NOT feel like

- Generic AI faceless content (stock cities, kinetic text, AI filler imagery)
- Entertainment-adjacent (thumbnail energy, emoji typography, shock-face)
- Academic or dry
- Conspiratorial (red strings, leaked-document textures, surveillance aesthetics)
- Decorative

## SECTION 2 — Documentary Hybrid Standard

**Validated:** Norway production cycle (OS-S-0003)

This is the primary visual language of Open Secret.

**AI creates atmosphere. Real artifacts carry information.**

| Layer | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **AI cinematic footage** | Establishes mood, place, scale | Establishing shots, abstract atmosphere, transitions |
| **Real artifacts** | Carries factual information | Documents, screenshots, interfaces, receipts, filings, labels |
| **Restrained typography** | Delivers the mechanism statement | Key numbers, reveal text |

### Documentary Hybrid rules

- Slow cinematic camera movement. No fast cuts in atmospheric footage.
- Minimal transitions between shots. Hard cuts preferred.
- Real documents and screenshots are used at their actual scale — not reconstructed.
- AI footage never carries legible factual information (no readable text, no logos).
- Typography appears only when the narration demands a visual anchor.

### Real Artifacts First

Priority order for every information-carrying visual:

1. **Licensed real artifact** — photograph, document, interface screenshot, product, receipt, filing, label, report
2. **Simplified reconstruction** derived from the real artifact
3. **Fully synthetic graphic** only if neither (1) nor (2) communicates the mechanism effectively

Do not reconstruct an object that already communicates the concept better in its real form.

## SECTION 3 — Color System

All colors carry semantic meaning. Color is never used for decoration or variety.

| Color | Hex | Semantic meaning | Usage rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Background** | `#0D1117` | All frames | Every frame |
| **Primary text** | `#EDEAE2` | All labels, narration text, subtitles | Always |
| **Money flow** | `#E8A838` | Amber | Money moving only — arrows, transaction nodes, revenue streams |
| **Profit / positive** | `#3DBD7D` | Green | Profit nodes, favorable outcomes, retention |
| **Cost / loss** | `#E05555` | Red | Cost nodes, loss scenarios, unfavorable comparisons |
| **Ownership / control** | `#4B7CE8` | Blue | Ownership lines, control relationships, structural entities |
| **Mechanism reveal** | `#A855F7` | Purple | **Once per episode maximum** — the single moment that IS the reveal |
| **Neutral / secondary** | `#6B7280` | Gray | Secondary labels, background structural info |
| **Container / frame** | `#1E2530` | Dark container | Diagram bounding boxes |

### Purple — the most important rule

Purple appears **at most once per episode**, at the exact moment the hidden mechanism is revealed. Never in the opening. Never repeated. The rarity is what makes it land.

If purple appears twice in one episode, it loses its function entirely.

### What colors do NOT mean

- Colors are not used for aesthetic variety across shots
- Colors are not used to distinguish different companies (use labels)
- A new color is never introduced without a new semantic assignment

## SECTION 4 — Typography

| Role | Font | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers / Headlines / Reveal text | Space Grotesk | ExtraBold |
| Labels / Body text | Inter | Medium |
| Annotations / Secondary | Inter | Regular |

### Size rules (mobile-first, 390px viewport)

| Element | Size |
|---|---|
| Display numbers (hook / reveal) | 80–90sp |
| Primary labels | 18–22sp |
| Secondary labels | 14–16sp |
| Subtitles | Inter Medium, max 8 words/line |

**Never below 36sp for any display element.** Never below 14sp for any label. Text must be legible on a phone screen in any lighting condition.

## SECTION 5 — Attention Layer: First 3 Seconds

**Core principle:** The first 3 seconds do not explain. They stop the scroll.

The explanation begins only after attention is secured.

**Rule (canonical, validated by 2 production cycles):** The first surprise — a number, comparison, contradiction, or reframe — must arrive within 3 seconds of the video starting. A recognition beat without a question is not a hook. 8 seconds of setup before the first surprise = swipe-away.

### 10 Hook Visual Patterns

**Pattern 1 — THE IMPOSSIBLE NUMBER**
A single large number fills most of the frame. Label appears 0.5 seconds later making the number feel impossible.
Example: `$1.50` → `Unchanged since 1985`
Rule: The number must genuinely surprise. If viewers already suspect the answer, this pattern fails.

**Pattern 2 — THE VANISHING MARGIN**
A full-width bar represents 100% revenue. The "cost" portion expands, compressing "profit" to a sliver — or eliminating it entirely.
Rule: The motion must feel like a discovery, not a demonstration.

**Pattern 3 — THE IMPOSSIBLE COMPARISON**
Two side-by-side numbers that should logically correlate — but don't.
Example: `REPORTED INCOME: $1 | NET WORTH: $150B`
Rule: Both numbers must be accurate and verifiable. The contrast must be genuine.

**Pattern 4 — THE REFRAME**
A familiar label appears, then changes to reveal the hidden classification.
Example: `coffee shop` → `real estate play`
Rule: The relabeling must be substantively accurate, not just clever.

**Pattern 5 — THE SILENT FLOW**
No text. Animated money flows for 2 seconds before labels appear.
Rule: The flow must be visually interpretable without labels.

**Pattern 6 — THE BEFORE/AFTER SPLIT**
Split screen. Left: visible surface. Right: actual structure. Both appear simultaneously.
Rule: Both sides use identical visual grammar. The structural contrast does the work.

**Pattern 7 — THE COUNTER RUNNING**
A running counter already mid-run when the video begins.
Rule: Number must be a real, verifiable rate (annual revenue converted to per-second).

**Pattern 8 — THE ARROW GOING THE WRONG WAY**
A single directional arrow pointing in the direction that violates the viewer's assumption.
Rule: The "wrong" direction must be genuinely counterintuitive.

**Pattern 9 — THE SHRINKING SHARE**
A bar fills to what looks like 60%... then stops at 2%. The rest fills with something unexpected.
Rule: The segment that shrinks must be the one the viewer expected to be large.

**Pattern 10 — THE MATCH**
Two numbers from unrelated categories appear simultaneously — and they're nearly identical. The equivalence IS the reveal.
Rule: The numbers must actually match. The power comes entirely from data accuracy.

## SECTION 6 — Motion Principles

**Motion communicates causality, sequence, and change.** Every animation must have a reason that can be stated in words.

### What motion is for

- Showing sequence: this happens, then this
- Showing causality: this causes this
- Directing attention: look here now
- Revealing: something previously hidden becomes visible

### What motion is NOT for

- Filling screen time
- Demonstrating production effort
- Making a diagram "feel premium"

### Rules

- **Hard cuts are the default transition.** 200ms cross-dissolve is the maximum.
- **No looping animations.** Diagrams build, hold, and clear. Looping signals "I ran out of content."
- **No zoom-in-zoom-out transitions** between elements in the same diagram. This is the single fastest way to undermine the premium register.
- **No ambient particle effects, background motion, or floating elements.**
- **Build-in behavior:** elements appear opacity 0→100 with slight upward translate (4–8px), 200–300ms.

### AI video motion language

In Documentary Hybrid, motion is primarily created through AI-generated cinematography:

- Slow push-in or pull-back (2–5% scale over the shot duration)
- Gentle pan following subject direction
- Static hold on key information moments — stillness = weight
- Hard cut between shots as the default

Avoid: whip-pans, rack focus effects, dramatic Dutch angles, time-lapse, speed ramps.

## SECTION 7 — The Five Recognizable Signatures

If the logo is removed, these five elements identify an Open Secret video within 5 seconds:

1. **Dark warm background (#0D1117) with warm off-white text** — not pure black, not navy, not generic dark-mode. Immediately distinctive.

2. **Amber money-flow color used only for one thing** — after 5 episodes, a viewer knows amber means "money is moving." The semantic discipline compounds.

3. **Purple reveal moment — once** — a viewer who has seen 5 episodes knows: when purple appears, the destination has been reached.

4. **Restrained typography over real or cinematic material** — not infographic animation. Not stock footage with text overlaid. Documentary-grade material with minimal, purposeful type.

5. **Two-register structure** — a hard attention-stop, then a calmer explanation. The gear-shift from "this is trying to get my attention" to "this is now explaining something" becomes recognizable.

## SECTION 8 — Publishing Technical Standards

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Resolution | 1080 × 1920 (9:16 vertical) |
| Target runtime | 58–59 seconds |
| Frame rate | 30fps |
| Export format | MP4 |
| Subtitle font | Inter Medium |
| Max subtitle words/line | 8 |
| Music | No Content ID risk (YouTube Audio Library or licensed) |

**58–59 seconds is a hard target.** Videos over 60 seconds do not qualify as YouTube Shorts and are processed as standard uploads — this eliminates Shorts discovery entirely.

**First frame = hook visual.** The first frame is the thumbnail in Shorts/Reels/TikTok. It must be a hook visual, not a neutral establishing shot.
