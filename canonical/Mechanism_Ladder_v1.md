# Mechanism Ladder

**Status:** Canonical Editorial Principle (Proposed)  
**Folder:** `00_Canonical`

---

## Purpose

Mechanism Ladder defines **which causal layer should become the first episode** and **how a mechanism should expand into a long-term content asset**.

Editorial depth and editorial quality are different variables.

The Editorial Board should optimize for the strongest first transformation, not the deepest explanation.

---

# Core Principle

Most hidden business systems contain multiple valid layers of causality.

Example:

**Level 1 — What is happening?**

Who really pays for your credit card rewards?

↓

**Level 2 — How does it happen?**

Retail pricing absorbs interchange costs.

↓

**Level 3 — Who designed the incentives?**

Visa and Mastercard designed rules that encourage reward competition.

↓

**Level 4 — Why is the system stable?**

Every participant benefits enough to remain inside it.

↓

**Level 5 — Why doesn't regulation change it?**

The cost of replacing the system exceeds the cost of preserving it.

Each level may deserve its own episode.

---

# Editorial Principle

The deepest mechanism is **not automatically** the strongest first episode.

Choose the earliest level that simultaneously maximizes:

- Curiosity
- Personal relevance
- T2 transformation
- Clarity
- Diagrammability
- Transferability

If a deeper mechanism reduces accessibility or requires excessive exposition, reserve it for a later episode.

---

# Editorial Planning

Mechanism Ladder has two independent purposes.

## 1. Episode Selection

Determine which causal layer creates the strongest first transformation.

## 2. Library Planning

Determine whether the mechanism should become:

- a standalone episode;
- a mini-series;
- a recurring franchise;
- an entire editorial pillar.

Do not compress an entire causal ladder into one episode if multiple levels independently satisfy the Constitution.

The objective is to maximize the lifetime value of the knowledge graph rather than the informational density of a single video.

---

# Backlog Rule

If Episode N intentionally leaves a deeper causal layer unexplained, the Editorial Board must explicitly create a high-priority backlog item for that next layer.

This preserves intellectual continuity without forcing consecutive publication.

---

# Editorial Warning

## Depth Bias

Editors naturally assume that discovering a deeper mechanism automatically creates a better episode.

This is false.

Depth and editorial strength are correlated but not identical.

The first episode should maximize transformation—not abstraction.

---

# Practical Test

When choosing between two valid reveals, ask:

> Does the deeper mechanism create a stronger first transformation?

If not:

Keep the simpler reveal for Episode 1.

Reserve the deeper mechanism for Episode 2 or later.
