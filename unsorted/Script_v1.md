# Script_v1.md — Why Does the Whole World Still Use Dollars?

**Implements:** Reveal_Brief_v1.md (approved). Hook = Impossible Divergence. Reveal = First-Mover Problem. Proof = Financing loop → Euro → RMB.
**Target runtime:** 55–65s. **Estimated runtime:** 57s.

---

Scene 01
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"America's share of world trade is shrinking. The dollar's grip on the world isn't."
Visual:
Two data trends appear together in the same frame — one falling, one holding steady or climbing. They're shown as a contradiction, not one after the other.
Purpose:
Create contradiction inside the 3-second hook window.

Scene 02
Duration: 3s
Voice-over:
"That's backwards."
Visual:
The gap between the two trends widens slightly, sharpening the tension already on screen.
Purpose:
Confirm the contradiction is real and hold the question open.

Scene 03
Duration: 3s
Voice-over:
"You'd think it's because America's economy is still the biggest."
Visual:
A "biggest / strongest" framing appears, sitting uneasily against the shrinking trend still visible from Scene 01.
Purpose:
Surface the first obvious explanation, ready to eliminate it.

Scene 04
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"Or maybe it's oil — priced in dollars for decades. But oil could vanish tomorrow, and this wouldn't move."
Visual:
Oil imagery appears, then fades out of frame entirely, while the dollar-dominance trend stays completely unaffected.
Purpose:
Eliminate the second obvious explanation in one beat, quickly.

Scene 05
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"So what's actually holding this up?"
Visual:
Pull back to a wide view — many countries or currencies, all connected through one central point. Nothing resolved yet.
Purpose:
Open the question fully; peak the curiosity gap before the reveal.

Scene 06
Duration: 3s
Voice-over:
"It's not trust."
Visual:
A trust or agreement symbol appears, then is visibly dismantled or struck through.
Purpose:
Begin the reveal by removing the wrong explanation first.

Scene 07
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"Nobody can afford to be the first to let go."
Visual:
The central network from Scene 05, now held still — every connected point visibly eyeing an exit, none of them moving. A single, distinct visual treatment marks this instant as the turning point of the film.
Purpose:
Land the core reveal. The purple moment.

Scene 08
Duration: 3s
Voice-over:
"Here's why leaving costs so much."
Visual:
Move from the wide network view into a closer, more mechanical view of a single connection.
Purpose:
Bridge from reveal to proof without losing momentum.

Scene 09
Duration: 3s
Voice-over:
"Most trade already runs through dollars."
Visual:
Global trade routes rendered overwhelmingly in one consistent visual treatment, showing concentration rather than variety.
Purpose:
Establish the first proof step — the sheer scale of existing use.

Scene 10
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"Which makes borrowing in dollars cheaper than almost anything else."
Visual:
A simple side-by-side comparison of borrowing cost across currencies. One is visibly, clearly lower than the rest.
Purpose:
Show the financial incentive created by that scale.

Scene 11
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"So the first country to switch gives up that advantage. Alone."
Visual:
One point detaches from the network, visibly losing its connection and its favorable position as it separates.
Purpose:
Close the financing-loop proof with a concrete cost.

Scene 12
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"Europe could build an alternative. It just doesn't have one thing — a single pool of debt deep enough for the world to trust."
Visual:
A strong, unified symbol for Europe that, on closer look, turns out to be made of several mismatched pieces rather than one solid whole.
Purpose:
Prove the trap holds even for a well-resourced challenger.

Scene 13
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"China could too. But that means giving up control over its own money — control it isn't ready to lose."
Visual:
A strong symbol for China's economic scale, paired with a gate or control point that stays firmly shut.
Purpose:
Prove the trap generalizes to a second, structurally different challenger.

Scene 14
Duration: 3s
Voice-over:
"So both just stay where they are."
Visual:
Both symbols from Scenes 12 and 13, held completely still.
Purpose:
Confirm the standstill before the final turn.

Scene 15
Duration: 4s
Voice-over:
"Every country holding dollars — including yours — is just waiting for someone else to move first."
Visual:
Return to the wide network view from Scene 07. Every point glances toward the others. None move.
Purpose:
Deliver the transfer lens and the personal stake together, as one resolving idea.

Scene 16
Duration: 3s
Voice-over:
"So nobody jumps."
Visual:
All motion in frame stops. The image holds in stillness.
Purpose:
Close on the mental model, not a summary. Inevitable, not dramatic.

## Self-Review

**Strongest moment:** Scene 07. It re-contextualizes a fact the viewer already accepts (the dollar dominates) rather than introducing a new one — which is exactly the "oh, that's why" mechanism the Reveal Brief specifies, not a "wait, what?" mechanism.

**Weakest moment:** Scene 04. It does two jobs in one beat — introducing the oil explanation and dismissing it — to satisfy the single-beat guardrail. It's the densest line in the script relative to the "one idea at a time" voice guideline, and worth an ear-check at the VO pass to confirm it doesn't rush.

**Where retention may dip:** Scenes 12–13. Two consecutive beats each introducing a new entity (Europe, then China) back to back is the point of highest explanatory load in the script. Visual differentiation between the two carries more weight here than anywhere else in the edit.

**Which scene carries the reveal:** Scene 07.

**Estimated runtime:** 57 seconds (16 beats).

**Confidence this script faithfully implements the Reveal Brief:** High. The six-stage architecture (hook → question → escalation → reveal → proof → resolution) is preserved in order; proof sequencing (financing loop, then Europe, then China) matches the locked weighting, with the financing loop occupying more cumulative screen time than Europe and China combined; the petrodollar gets exactly one dismissive beat with no historical exposition; no third or fourth challenger currency appears; the ending closes on the mental model rather than a prediction; and the personal stake is folded into Scene 15 as a brief phrase rather than a separate beat, per the brief's own instruction.
