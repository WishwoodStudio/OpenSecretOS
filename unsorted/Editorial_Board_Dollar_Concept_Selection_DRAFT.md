# Editorial Board — Concept Selection: Dollar Dominance Episode

**Status:** DRAFT — for 04_Research. Editorial Board comparative pass. No new research; synthesizes Round 1, Round 2, Editorial Review, and canonical documents.
**Governance note:** This is a recommendation, not a ratification. Reveal Ratification and the Selection Gate are Eugene's per Decision Log v2; Claude does not self-ratify either. IC (ChatGPT) adversarial pass still outstanding.
**Objective:** Determine which of three editorial concepts produces the strongest episode.

---

# CANDIDATE A — The Coordination Problem

**1. Editorial Promise**
Show the viewer that the dollar is not really a currency competition at all — it's a shared standard, and standards don't win on merit.

**2. Prior Belief**
The dollar is the world's money because it's the best money — the most trusted, most stable, backed by the strongest economy. Currencies compete, and this one is winning.

**3. Belief Transformation**
The viewer stops seeing the dollar as a *winner* and starts seeing it as *infrastructure* — a default that got adopted, not a contest that got won.

**4. Core Reveal** *(20 words max)*
The dollar isn't the world's best currency. It's the world's default setting — and defaults never win on quality.

**5. Proof**
- Invoicing and transaction dominance vastly exceed the US share of world trade — usage is untethered from the underlying economy.
- The same pattern appears in every standard that isn't the best option (shipping containers, keyboard layouts, time zones) — adoption, not merit, decides.
- Reserve share and transaction share have moved in *opposite* directions, which a merit-based story cannot produce.

**6. Emotional Payoff**
Recognition, shading into mild irony — "of course, it's the same as every other standard nobody actually chose."

**7. Visual Architecture**
1. Dollar presented as a familiar object of trust
2. Reframe: dollar as a plug/socket/standard, not a competitor
3. Analogy tile — other standards that won without being best
4. The usage-vs-economy gap made visible
5. Landing on "default, not winner"

**8. Risks**
The reference viewer (business-literate adult, Constitution §6) *already knows about network effects*. Belief distance is therefore short — this risks being a well-made restatement of something the target audience can already articulate. It also sits closest to the tautology Round 2 rejected: without extreme discipline, "it's the standard because it's the standard" resurfaces. Weakest T2 (Violation) of the three.

# CANDIDATE B — The Impossible Divergence

**1. Editorial Promise**
Show the viewer two lines that should move together, and don't — then explain the gap.

**2. Prior Belief**
Currency power tracks economic power. If America's share of the world economy shrinks, the dollar's grip should loosen with it.

**3. Belief Transformation**
The viewer learns that a currency's global dominance has *detached* from the size of the economy behind it — the two are no longer the same variable.

**4. Core Reveal** *(20 words max)*
America's slice of world trade keeps shrinking. The dollar's grip keeps tightening. Currency power stopped tracking economic power.

**5. Proof**
- Dollar sits on one side of 89.2% of all FX trades (2025) — up from 88.4% in 2022, while the US share of world trade fell.
- Dollar invoicing runs at a large multiple of the US share of world trade and has risen as that share declined.
- Reserve share (~57%, a 30-year low) moving opposite to transaction share proves these are separate variables, not one story.

**6. Emotional Payoff**
Surprise, then unease — the sense that a familiar rule of thumb has quietly stopped being true.

**7. Visual Architecture**
1. Two lines introduced as obviously coupled
2. Time advances — they diverge
3. The gap isolated and named
4. Three metrics separated so the divergence is unmistakable
5. Landing on decoupling — dominance without proportional weight

**8. Risks**
Highest G2 (Coherent) exposure of the three. "America is shrinking while the dollar tightens" is one adjective away from decline-of-America commentary, which §11 excludes outright. It also carries the heaviest factual-precision burden: the episode must keep reserve share, transaction share, and invoicing share visibly distinct (Round 1 §8 conflation risk) or the reveal collapses into a misleading claim. Most importantly — **B is a question, not an answer.** Its reveal states *that* the lines diverge; the viewer still leaves asking *why*, which means either the episode ends unresolved or it must import another concept's mechanism to close.

# CANDIDATE C — The First-Mover Problem

**1. Editorial Promise**
Explain why a system that everyone could leave, and that no one particularly loves, refuses to move.

**2. Prior Belief**
Countries hold dollars because they trust America most. If they stopped trusting it, they'd switch.

**3. Belief Transformation**
The viewer stops reading dollar dominance as a verdict on America and starts reading it as a trap in which everyone is waiting on everyone else.

**4. Core Reveal** *(20 words max)*
Nobody's holding dollars because they trust America. They're holding them because no one can afford to let go first.

**5. Proof**
- Leaving alone is measurably expensive: dollar invoicing makes dollar borrowing cheaper, so the first mover surrenders that advantage while everyone else keeps it.
- The euro has the scale but not the unified safe asset — closing that gap requires fiscal pooling member states have repeatedly declined.
- The yuan has the trade volume and the payment rails but not open convertibility — and Beijing has chosen capital control over internationalization.

**6. Emotional Payoff**
Recognition — the specific pleasure of seeing a familiar human dynamic (nobody leaves the party first) operating at the scale of the world economy.

**7. Visual Architecture**
1. The assumption: dollar held out of trust
2. Everyone eyeing the exit — nobody moving
3. The cost of moving first, isolated
4. Two challengers, each missing one nameable thing they've chosen not to build
5. Landing on the standoff — everyone waiting on everyone

**8. Risks**
Tone drift is the live danger: "trap" must read as *engineered structure*, never as nations being victimized by the dollar (Constitution §7). The euro/yuan proof beat also brushes against political commentary and must stay strictly mechanical — what's missing, not who's to blame. Weaker cold-open than B: the first-mover idea needs a beat of setup before it stops the scroll, which puts direct pressure on the 3-second Hook Rule.

# COMPARATIVE REVIEW

*Scoring note: for Production Complexity, higher = simpler to produce.*

| Criterion | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scroll stopping | 4 | **9** | 6 |
| Curiosity | 5 | **9** | 7 |
| Belief transformation | 4 | 8 | **9** |
| Memorability | 4 | 6 | **9** |
| Visual simplicity | 6 | **8** | **8** |
| Emotional payoff | 3 | 6 | **9** |
| Franchise potential | 6 | 7 | **8** |
| Production complexity (higher = simpler) | 7 | 6 | **8** |

**Scroll stopping** — B maps directly onto two validated hook patterns (Impossible Comparison, Arrow Going the Wrong Way) and opens on a number. C needs a sentence of setup first. A opens on an abstraction, which is the weakest possible cold open.

**Curiosity** — B manufactures an unanswered "why" in the first two seconds, which is the purest form of the curiosity gap. C's question is compelling but arrives slightly later. A's question is one the target viewer half-answers themselves.

**Belief transformation** — C replaces a *causal* belief (trust → dominance) with a different causal model, and the new model is portable. B corrects a real belief but a narrower one. A's target belief is one the reference viewer often already holds.

**Memorability** — C exits with a portable sentence a viewer can repeat at dinner. B exits with a chart — vivid but harder to retell. A exits with a category label.

**Visual simplicity** — B is two lines, which is as clean as it gets, though it needs careful metric labeling. C is one metaphor plus one comparison. A needs an analogy stack to carry an abstract point.

**Emotional payoff** — C triggers recognition, the strongest available feeling here because the viewer has personally lived the dynamic. B delivers surprise but stays analytical. A lands closer to "noted" than "felt."

**Franchise potential** — C opens both the monetary ladder (safe assets → eurodollar → de-dollarization, per Round 1 Step 4) *and* a recurring mechanism family (first-mover traps appear across standards, industries, and platforms). B is a repeatable *format* rather than a content ladder. A is a mid-sized family.

**Production complexity** — C leans on metaphor and atmosphere, which the Documentary Hybrid handles natively. B demands data visualization precise enough to survive scrutiny across three easily-confused metrics. A needs the most invented visual material, which pushes against Real Artifacts First.

# INVESTMENT COMMITTEE DECISION

**Recommendation: Candidate C — The First-Mover Problem.**

B wins the scoreboard on attention and loses on the thing the channel actually sells. The Channel Promise is transformation — "the viewer should leave each video unable to look at the topic the same way again" — and Editorial Handbook §2 makes belief distance, not interest, the primary success metric. B leaves the viewer with a *fact about the world* (two lines diverged). C leaves them with a *tool for reading the world*: once a viewer understands that systems persist because no participant can afford to move first, they start seeing it in industry standards, in software nobody likes, in their own workplace. That is the transfer lens, and it is the difference between an episode people find interesting and an episode that changes how they explain things — which is the stated mission.

C also carries the strongest long-term brand asset: it opens two independent franchise directions at once, and it produces the recurring register Open Secret wants — wonder and structural inevitability rather than exposure or accusation. B, by contrast, sits permanently one word away from decline-of-America framing, and a channel that builds its most-viewed episode on that frame inherits an audience expecting more of it. That is a brand risk that compounds.

**Critical qualifier:** B should not be discarded — it should be *absorbed*. B's divergence is the strongest available opening for C. The board's finding is that these two concepts are not really competing at the same altitude: B is the best cold open, C is the best episode. Recommended construction is B's diverging lines as the hook and escalation, resolving into C's reveal. This directly addresses C's only serious weakness (a soft cold open against the 3-second Hook Rule) using material already researched and sourced. A is retired as a standalone concept; its "default, not winner" framing may survive as a single line inside C.

# ASSUME WE ARE WRONG

The strongest case against C is that we have optimized for the wrong stage of the channel's life. Open Secret has two published episodes and effectively no audience, and Decision Log v2 records that discovery-stage weighting — broad recognition, curiosity, click potential — is the *currently active, ratified* topic-selection posture. Under that posture, B's decisively better cold open is not a secondary criterion; it is the binding constraint, and a more memorable episode that fewer people finish is worth less right now than a slightly shallower one that stops the scroll cold. There is also a real risk that C's recognition payoff is softer than it looks on paper: the "nobody leaves first" dynamic may read to a business-literate viewer as something they already understand intuitively, in which case C's apparent advantage in belief transformation evaporates and B's hard data advantage is all that's left.

Concrete evidence that would prove C was the wrong call: if, during scripting, the first-mover reveal cannot be stated without first explaining the financing loop — meaning the proof consumes the runtime and the reveal arrives late, breaking the Rolling Payoff Rule; if the trap framing keeps drifting into victim register across revisions and fails the §7 tone check repeatedly rather than occasionally; if the euro/yuan proof beat cannot be compressed without either becoming politically framed or becoming too thin to prove anything; or, post-publication, if retention data shows viewers dropping *before* the reveal beat, which would indicate the hook was the real constraint all along and B should have been the center rather than the opening. A fifth, quieter signal: if test readers can restate the divergence but not the trap, the transformation we believe C delivers isn't actually landing.

**Open gate, all three concepts:** T5 (Personal Stake) remains unsolved. No round has produced a concrete personal consequence, and Governance Rule 9 makes this a hard gate at script lock. This must be answered before the Reveal Brief is ratified, whichever concept wins.
